WHO LENDS?
The regulated entity (RE)—for example a bank or NBFC within RBI's jurisdiction—is the lender of record and owns regulatory lending obligations. A digital lending app (DLA) is a mobile/web interface. A lending service provider (LSP) may acquire customers, provide underwriting support, servicing, monitoring or recovery functions as an agent. A product's brand/app is not proof it is itself the lender. Display the RE, agreement, key terms, grievance owner and flow of funds clearly.

LIFECYCLE AND SYSTEMS
1. Discovery/consent: app presents offers, lender identity, eligibility/disclosures and privacy notices; customer gives separate necessary permissions. Avoid dark patterns and unnecessary data collection.
2. Application: collect minimum required identity, contact, income, employment/business and requested-loan inputs. Record source, consent, timestamp and purpose. A customer can correct data.
3. KYC and fraud checks: RE performs applicable CDD; device/identity and document checks support but do not replace legal KYC. Consent-based financial data may arrive via Account Aggregator; see 09.
4. Underwriting: decision engine estimates repayment ability, credit risk, policy eligibility, exposure, pricing and limits. Automated score is a decision support/control, not a legal guarantee. Store versioned features, decision reasons and human overrides as required by policy.
5. Sanction: lender decides approved principal, rate type, fees, tenor, schedule, collateral/guarantee, conditions and expiry. Customer receives contract and Key Fact Statement (KFS) under applicable rules. APR represents annualized all-inclusive loan cost as defined by current directions; actual quote depends on calculation method and disclosed charges.
6. Acceptance/e-sign: customer accepts the lender's terms; contract version and authentication evidence are retained.
7. Disbursal: lender transfers funds to borrower or permitted end-beneficiary via regulated account flow. RBI's Digital Lending Directions specify funds-flow/customer-protection conditions; do not send disbursal to an LSP pool account except where a stated exception applies.
8. Servicing: LMS records principal, accruals, due schedule, payment allocations, statements, queries, rate resets, prepayment, mandate status and fees.
9. Repayment: payment comes through UPI/NACH/card/netbanking/other contracted route; rail status is reconciled to loan account. Debit event alone does not prove LMS allocation.
10. Collections/closure: due reminders, grace/overdue classification, authorized collection actions, complaint resolution, prepayment quote, final settlement and no-dues/charge release follow contract and rules.

LOS AND LMS
Loan Origination System (LOS) manages application, documentation, KYC, credit decision, offers, sanction and disbursal handoff. Loan Management System (LMS) manages booked facility, schedule, accrual, due/receipt allocation, delinquency, modification, recovery, closure and statements. A credit decision platform may be a component of LOS or external. Source of truth for contractual schedule should be explicit and reconciled with accounting/GL.

EMI EXAMPLE (REDUCING-BALANCE FORMULA)
For principal P, monthly periodic rate r and n months, EMI = P*r*(1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n - 1). This assumes fixed periodic rate and equal end-period installments; fees, day-count, floating resets, rounding and lender contract can differ. Example P=₹120,000, nominal annual rate 12%, monthly r=0.01, n=12 -> EMI about ₹10,661.85. Total scheduled payments about ₹127,942.20; interest about ₹7,942.20 before fees and rounding. First month interest ₹1,200, principal about ₹9,461.85, closing principal about ₹110,538.15. A production LMS uses exact schedule conventions and currency rounding.

PAYMENT ALLOCATION AND DELINQUENCY
An EMI receipt can be allocated among charges, penal charges where permitted, interest and principal according to contract and law. Partial/late receipt creates a different state from payment initiation. If a ₹10,662 debit is pending but not settled, LMS may remain due; after confirmed receipt it applies amount and recalculates outstanding. Delinquency bucket is based on due date/payment history under applicable rules. Non-performing asset (NPA) classification and provisioning follow lender category and current RBI prudential directions; do not invent a universal day count without checking current lender-specific applicability.

FAILURES AND CONTROLS
Duplicate loan application; identity mismatch; stale bank statement; consent expired; bureau unavailable; wrong account; duplicate disbursal; mandate inactive; payment debit with no loan-credit; split payment; reversed collection; statement mismatch; fraud model false positive; collection-agent complaint. Use idempotent disbursal IDs, approval state machine, direct settlement trace, daily repayment-to-LMS reconciliation and immutable schedule revisions. Do not re-disburse because a timeout alone leaves outcome unknown.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS
An app is not necessarily the lender. An approval screen is not disbursal. A repayment app status is not necessarily a loan-ledger posting. A write-off does not automatically cancel a borrower's legal obligation. APR is not simply nominal interest; it is a disclosed annualized cost measure under the applicable computation framework.
