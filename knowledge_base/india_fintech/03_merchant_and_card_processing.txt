MERCHANT ACCEPTANCE ROLES
Merchant: sells goods/services, creates order and fulfillment records.
Acquiring bank/provider: connects merchant to one or more payment rails and receives/settles obligations as contracted.
Payment Gateway (PG): typically provides technical capture, encryption/tokenization, routing and status interfaces. It may not receive or hold funds.
Payment Aggregator (PA): under RBI's Indian payment-system framework, aggregates merchant acceptance and may receive/pool customer funds for onward settlement; authorization and escrow/settlement obligations depend on current RBI directions. Do not call every gateway a PA or assume the same funds flow.
Issuer: customer's card/PPI issuing bank or institution. Card network routes messages and applies scheme rules. Processor/PSP is a broad technical or service role defined by contract and rail.

MERCHANT ONBOARDING WORKFLOW
1. Merchant submits legal entity, beneficial owner, bank settlement details, website/app, business model, products, expected volume and customer-support details to its PA/acquirer.
2. Provider performs due diligence and risk/merchant category assessment under its regulated role, scheme rules, AML/CFT controls and current RBI directions.
3. Contract sets fees, reserve/hold rights, payout timing, refund/dispute process, prohibited activities, chargeback evidence and data obligations.
4. Integration credentials are issued per environment. Test/sandbox keys differ from production keys; secrets must not enter client-side code or logs.
5. Merchant maps order identifiers to payment intent, authorization, capture, refund, payout and dispute references. A paid order must be reconciled to settlement, not inferred from browser redirect alone.

CARD PURCHASE LIFECYCLE
1. Checkout creates an order and payment attempt with a unique merchant order ID and idempotency key.
2. Gateway/acquirer routes card authorization via the network to issuer. E-commerce may use tokenized card credentials and 3-D Secure / additional factor authentication under applicable rules. Authentication helps verify identity; it does not guarantee issuer approval.
3. Issuer checks card/account status, available credit/funds, authentication, limits and risk; returns approve or decline plus network reference. Approval often creates a hold/reservation, not final merchant payment.
4. Merchant captures an approved transaction at purchase or later. Partial capture/void behavior is contract/scheme dependent.
5. Acquirer batches clearing records through network; issuer/acquirer obligations are settled through the network's settlement process.
6. Acquirer/PA pays merchant based on contract, schedule, deductions, refunds, reserves and risk review. Merchant reconciles payout file to captured sales and bank credit.

SYNCHRONOUS ACK, ASYNC CALLBACK, WEBHOOK, POLLING
An API request may return `accepted` with a transaction ID before issuer result is known. A webhook can later report success/failure, but callbacks can be delayed, repeated or arrive out of order. Validate webhook signature and timestamp; deduplicate with provider event ID; fetch authoritative status when outcome is uncertain; reconcile later against settlement/statement data. A browser redirect is not an authoritative payment record. See 06 and 10.

WALLETS AND PPIs
A prepaid payment instrument (PPI) stores value or represents a prepaid claim usable under the issuer's product/rules. Wallets are software interfaces for PPIs and/or linked payment methods; “wallet” is not one regulatory type. Closed-system instruments usable only with issuer are treated differently from semi-closed/open PPIs under RBI directions. Full-KYC, small PPIs, interoperability, limits, refunds and cross-border functionality are subject to RBI PPI Directions as amended. Do not quote an old limit from a stale blog. A wallet ledger, issuer settlement account and merchant payout ledger are distinct records.
RBI MD-PPIs consolidated as of 27 Dec 2024: https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12156

PRICING AND MERCHANT STATEMENTS
MDR (merchant discount rate) is a merchant-side fee concept, not a universal fee for every UPI/card/rail payment. Merchant net settlement may equal captured gross sales less refunds, chargebacks, fees, taxes, reserves, prior adjustments and other contractually permitted items. A sample payout: gross captured ₹50,000; refunds ₹2,000; fee/charges ₹600; reserve ₹1,000 -> illustrative net ₹46,400. Actual fee bases/tax treatment follow contract and applicable law.

REFUND AND CHARGEBACK PATHS
Refund: merchant initiates a credit linked to original payment; PA/acquirer/network routes it; issuer posts credit; merchant ledger and payout reconciliation record both gross sale and refund. It can take time to appear in card/account record.
Chargeback/dispute: customer contacts issuer, issuer raises scheme case, acquirer/merchant submits evidence, network rules determine representment/decision and financial adjustment. It is not the same as a voluntary refund or immediate reversal. Preserve order, delivery, consent/auth, transaction and communication evidence with retention controls.

SECURITY CONTROLS
Use hosted fields/tokenization where appropriate; never store CVV after authorization or log full PAN/OTP/PIN. Scope payment-card data environment, apply least privilege, TLS, encryption, key rotation, fraud velocity rules, device/identity risk controls, secure webhook validation and PCI DSS where applicable to the service. Indian RBI card-data storage and tokenization rules must be checked in current official directions; do not infer that tokenization removes every compliance duty.

FAILURE CASE: CUSTOMER DEBITED, MERCHANT SAYS FAILED
Check whether there is an authorization hold, captured/posting transaction, completed rail transaction, or a timeout with unknown status. Match provider payment ID to merchant order and bank RRN/UTR. Query provider status before retrying; if the original completed, fulfill/reconcile or refund according to contract. If failed but funds are debited, follow rail-specific RBI TAT/dispute route from 04. Never assume the same deadlines for cards, UPI and bank transfer.
