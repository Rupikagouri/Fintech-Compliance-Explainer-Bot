MESSAGE IS NOT MONEY
SWIFT is a financial messaging network/standard ecosystem; a SWIFT/BIC identifies an institution/address, not a deposit or transfer by itself. A cross-border payment can involve instruction messages, correspondent accounts, FX conversion, sanctions/AML controls, settlement through one or more banks, and beneficiary posting. Each intermediary can have its own reference and fee.

CORRESPONDENT BANKING TERMS
Nostro: our account held with another bank, described from the account-owning bank's perspective. Vostro: your account held with us, from receiving bank perspective. Correspondent bank supplies access to currency/market/payment system where the sender or recipient bank lacks direct membership. Intermediary deductions, cut-off calendars, holidays, compliance review and FX value dates can affect amount/timing.

SIMPLIFIED OUTWARD REMITTANCE FLOW
Indian customer instructs bank/authorized channel with beneficiary, bank identifiers, amount/currency, purpose and required declarations -> authorized dealer (AD) bank validates identity, purpose, available permissions, documentation, limits/tax collection obligations applicable at that time -> bank quotes FX rate/spread/fees and debits INR -> bank or partner sends international payment instruction through correspondent/network -> intermediary institutions screen and settle -> beneficiary bank credits foreign-currency account or returns payment -> sender receives final status/foreign reference -> bank reconciles nostro statement, fees, FX and regulatory reporting. The exact legal route varies by remittance purpose and product.

INWARD REMITTANCE FLOW
Foreign remitter sends through partner/bank -> correspondent/AD bank receives foreign currency and message -> compliance/purpose checks and FX conversion if customer wants INR -> beneficiary identity/account validated -> INR credit posted or clarification/return generated -> inward reference and advice shared -> bank reconciles foreign nostro and fees. Receipt into account is different from message arrival.

FX PRICE AND EXAMPLE
If a provider sells USD 100 at an illustrative ₹83.40/USD and charges ₹250 disclosed fee, gross INR debit is ₹8,340 + ₹250 = ₹8,590 before any other applicable tax/charges. Actual customer rate may include spread from market reference; provider must disclose its applied rate/fees as required by product rules. Exchange rates move; example is not current quote.

PA-CB AND E-COMMERCE
RBI's 31 Oct 2023 circular brings entities facilitating cross-border payment transactions for import/export of goods/services under PA-CB (Payment Aggregator–Cross Border) directions, with role-specific due diligence, registration/authorization and settlement requirements. Domestic PA, PG, AD bank, exporter/importer and PA-CB may be separate. The PA-CB must not be confused with international card network or SWIFT. Confirm current amended RBI direction and authorization status before mapping a production flow.
Official circular: https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12561

FAILURES AND OPERATIONS
Wrong beneficiary/intermediary bank; missing purpose/document; invalid account; sanctions/name-screen alert; correspondent rejection; intermediary fee; FX value-date mismatch; returned payment; network timeout; duplicate instruction; partial beneficiary credit (where product allows). Preserve sender's instruction ID, UETR or bank reference where provided, correspondent refs, currency/amounts, FX quote, beneficiary details and return reason. Query status before resubmitting, reconcile foreign statement and INR account, and escalate through AD bank. Do not promise universal reversal time.

COMPLIANCE CAUTION
FEMA, RBI AD-bank directions, remittance schemes, sanctions, tax collection/withholding and purpose documentation are specific to transaction type, residency, amount, entity and effective date. This document deliberately avoids stating current LRS thresholds/tax rates. Verify current RBI/FEMA/Income Tax official material for live requirements; no generic “all remittances are allowed” answer is safe.
