WHAT AN ACCOUNT AGGREGATOR DOES
An Account Aggregator (AA) is a consent manager/data-transfer intermediary in India's regulated financial information-sharing framework. It obtains/manages customer consent and facilitates secure transfer of specified financial information from a Financial Information Provider (FIP) to a Financial Information User (FIU). It is not a credit bureau, a lender, a bank account, a wallet, or a general-purpose data broker. The AA framework is designed so the customer's information is transferred under a consent artefact with purpose, data type, recipient, date/time/expiry and other defined attributes. An AA should not ask/store customer credentials that authenticate the customer to an FIP.

PARTICIPANTS
Customer/data principal; FIP (bank, insurer, pension, securities or other participating institution as covered); AA; FIU (regulated lender or another eligible user); consent manager interface; FIP/FIU adapters; security/tokenization and audit services. The legal role and permitted data classes depend on the current RBI directions and ecosystem participation.

CONSENT-BASED DATA FLOW
1. FIU explains purpose (for example, assess a loan request) and identifies requested data classes, date range, frequency, recipient and retention/usage basis.
2. Customer selects FIP accounts and reviews consent artefact: what data, why, who receives it, how often, start/end, revoke/expire options.
3. Customer authenticates directly through FIP's approved user journey; AA does not collect the FIP password/PIN.
4. FIP verifies authorization, packages requested data and sends it securely through the AA; in standard AA architecture the AA facilitates transport and should not use the data for its own unrelated purpose.
5. FIU receives data, validates provenance, completeness, timestamps and units, then uses only for disclosed purpose and applicable legal basis. It stores minimum required records and logs data access/decision lineage.
6. Customer can inspect/revoke future consent where framework permits; prior lawful processing and record-retention obligations may still remain. Revocation does not necessarily erase information already lawfully received or a lender's required loan records.

CONSENT ARTEFACT VERSUS API TOKEN
Consent artefact records customer authorization and the scope/purpose of a financial data flow. An access token/API credential authenticates a software call. A valid access token does not create customer consent; a consent artefact alone does not authenticate an API client. Both are needed, along with FIP authorization and technical security.

EXAMPLE: CASH-FLOW UNDERWRITING
A borrower requests a business loan. Lender's FIU asks for consent to obtain six months of selected current-account transaction data from two FIPs, for credit assessment, one-time fetch, with stated recipient. Borrower approves through FIP flows. AA transports data; lender parses credits/debits, marks reversals and internal transfers, checks source/period completeness, derives cash-flow features and stores decision trace. AA does not decide credit. Lender remains accountable for underwriting and KFS/disclosure under lending rules.

EMBEDDED FINANCE
Embedded finance presents a financial function inside a nonfinancial journey (e.g. checkout loan offer, insurance quote in travel booking, business settlement account in marketplace). The host app provides distribution/interface; licensed bank/NBFC/insurer/payment entity remains responsible for regulated product decisions within its role. Integration needs entity disclosure, consent, data minimization, API security, product suitability/process controls, transparent fees, complaint routing, and explicit system-of-record boundaries. “Embedded” does not mean regulation is embedded in the retailer or delegated away.

TECHNICAL AND OPERATIONAL CONTROLS
Consent ID, purpose, data class, account handle, request ID, FIP/FIU/AA IDs, period, timestamps, expiry, revocation, token, schema version, data provenance and fetch status are traceable. Idempotently handle retries; encrypt payloads; verify identities and signatures; alert on overbroad/expired consent; prevent use outside purpose; maintain deletion/retention controls; avoid credentials; test partial data, stale records, duplicate delivery and revocation races. Financial data is not always accurate or sufficient to infer income/creditworthiness—model validation and explainability remain necessary.
