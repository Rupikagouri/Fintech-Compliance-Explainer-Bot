COMMON MODEL
A payment instruction and a movement of funds are related but different. The customer channel constructs an instruction; the sending institution authenticates/validates; a rail routes and records messages; participant institutions apply debit/credit rules; clearing and settlement discharge inter-participant obligations; each institution posts its customer ledger; status acknowledgements propagate back. Some steps are near-real-time, others are batched. A timeout means the app lacks a final response, not that the payment definitely failed. Names, message formats, retry behavior and internal bank stages vary. The sequences below are simplified, not proprietary specifications.

UPI — INSTANT ACCOUNT-TO-ACCOUNT PUSH/COLLECT
Purpose: mobile-first person-to-person and merchant payments using a UPI ID/VPA, QR, account details, or permitted linked credentials. Participants include payer app (bank app or TPAP through a PSP bank), payer bank, NPCI UPI switch, payee bank, and potentially merchant acquirer/PA for merchant acceptance.
Prerequisites: eligible bank account, supported app/device binding, mobile registration as required by the bank, UPI registration, beneficiary/merchant address, and payer authorization. UPI PIN authorizes a debit; never disclose it to receive money.
Simplified path: payer selects payee/QR and amount -> app forms transaction with payer/payee identifiers and reference -> customer approves using bank-authorized factor (commonly UPI PIN for debit) -> PSP routes request to NPCI -> payer bank validates account/auth/risk/balance and debits or declines -> switch routes to payee bank -> payee bank attempts credit -> status responses return across participants -> apps/merchant reconcile final state. Exact ordering and internal posting controls vary; the network message itself is not a customer ledger.
Money: payer account debit and payee account credit occur on bank ledgers; NPCI scheme settlement between participants is distinct from those entries and can follow scheme-defined cycles. A merchant acquiring/payout contract can add another settlement leg.
Exceptions: timeout, payer debit but no credit, duplicate callback, wrong VPA, bank unavailable, risk decline, merchant order mismatch. Record UTR/reference; query original status; do not blindly create a second payment. See 04 and 05.
Example: Asha buys ₹1,000 of groceries by dynamic QR. She checks the merchant name and amount, approves in her app; payer bank debits ₹1,000, payee bank credits the merchant-linked account, merchant order records payment. If the response times out after debit, the state is “unknown/pending” until rail/bank status resolves; the shop's order page and bank statement can temporarily differ.

IMPS — IMMEDIATE INTERBANK TRANSFER
NPCI describes IMPS as available round-the-clock and publishes a ₹5 lakh per-transaction ceiling for ordinary channels except SMS/IVR; bank/channel limits can be lower. Customer initiates through supported mobile/internet/ATM channel using account+IFSC or supported mobile-linked addressing. Sending bank authenticates and validates -> IMPS switch routes -> receiving bank posts or rejects -> response returns. Money movement, participant settlement, and customer status should be reconciled separately. Failed debited-but-not-credited cases have a rail-specific RBI turnaround framework; do not reuse UPI merchant deadlines.
Official: https://www.npci.org.in/product/imps

NEFT — RBI-OPERATED BATCH RETAIL TRANSFER
Customer gives beneficiary name, account number, IFSC, amount. Remitting bank validates/instructs -> RBI NEFT processes in half-hourly batches and is available 24x7x365 per FAQ -> beneficiary bank receives settled instruction and credits or returns -> remitting customer receives confirmation/reference. RBI's FAQ says credit or return is expected within two hours of batch settlement. NEFT has no RBI-set floor/ceiling, but banks may set channel limits. Batch timing is not “one hour banking-hours only.”
Example: ₹18,500 rent transfer is accepted just after a batch cutoff. The app may show submitted while the instruction waits for a subsequent batch. A UTR helps bank support locate it.
Official: https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/FAQView.aspx?Id=60

RTGS — REAL-TIME GROSS INTERBANK SETTLEMENT
RTGS settles qualifying transfers individually in central-bank money, transaction by transaction without netting. The RBI FAQ states 24x7x365 availability, minimum ₹2 lakh, no RBI upper amount cap, and beneficiary credit within 30 minutes after the beneficiary bank receives the transfer message. Bank channel limits may differ. Remitter submits beneficiary details -> remitting bank validates -> RTGS processes/settles -> beneficiary bank credits or returns -> positive confirmation goes to remitting bank. RTGS finality at interbank settlement does not make every user-interface update instantaneous.
Example: a business sends ₹4.2 lakh to a supplier. It saves the 22-character UTR, confirms debit, and asks the remitting bank to trace if the supplier's bank does not show credit.
Official: https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/FS_FAQs.aspx?Id=65

AePS — AADHAAR ENABLED PAYMENT SYSTEM
AePS enables specified interoperable, Aadhaar-authenticated banking services through business correspondents/micro-ATMs. Roles can include customer, BC agent, acquiring bank, issuer/account-holding bank, NPCI AePS switch, and Aadhaar authentication infrastructure. A customer requests supported transaction; agent device captures required transaction details and approved authentication input; acquiring participant routes request; issuer bank validates account/service and response; cash is handed to customer only after authorized success; agent settlement and cash/reconciliation controls follow participant rules. Biometric authentication is not the same as account debit consent for every possible purpose; supported transactions and consent are bounded by the applicable system flow. Protect biometrics and receipts; failed cash-dispense versus debit cases need separate complaint handling.
Official references: https://www.npci.org.in/product/aeps and UIDAI https://uidai.gov.in/

NACH — BULK AND RECURRING CLEARING
National Automated Clearing House supports bulk credit and debit flows and mandate-based recurring collections. Sponsor/utility institution submits a validated file or transaction set -> NPCI clearing processes it under the relevant cycle -> destination banks post accepted items or return rejects -> sponsor/beneficiary reconciles acknowledgements and settlement. Common fintech uses include salary/benefit credits and loan EMI mandates. A mandate is authority for a defined recurring debit, not evidence every debit succeeded. File control totals, item counts, mandate status, return reason and settlement totals should be reconciled.
Official: https://www.npci.org.in/what-we-do/nach/product-overview

BBPS — INTEROPERABLE BILL PAYMENT
Bharat Bill Payment System is an interoperable bill-payment ecosystem. A customer-facing operating unit/channel accepts biller category, consumer/account reference and amount; bill payment operating units, BBPCU and participating agents/billers route and settle under BBPS rules. A valid bill payment confirmation/reference should be persisted separately from the later biller ledger update. Errors include invalid consumer number, stale bill, timeout, duplicate presentment, delayed confirmation and refund/reversal. Use the BBPS transaction reference and channel complaint process; do not assume every biller has identical posting latency.
Official RBI BBPS Directions landing: https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasterDirections.aspx ; NPCI BBPS: https://www.npci.org.in/what-we-do/bharat-billpay

NETC FASTag — TOLL PAYMENTS
NETC is NPCI's interoperable electronic toll collection system. A vehicle's RFID FASTag is read at a toll plaza, transaction data is routed across acquirer/issuer/system participants, the linked wallet/PPI or account funding arrangement is debited, plaza receives transaction status, and clearing/settlement and exceptions follow NETC rules. Vehicle/Tag status, insufficient balance, blacklisting, duplicate reads and toll-lane communication failures can change the outcome. Preserve plaza, timestamp, tag and transaction references and use issuer support. NPCI describes a common interoperable process and clearing-house services; internal vendor protocols vary.
Official: https://www.npci.org.in/product/netc/about-netc
