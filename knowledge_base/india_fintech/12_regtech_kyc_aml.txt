CONTROL CATEGORIES THAT MUST NOT BE COLLAPSED
Customer Due Diligence (CDD/KYC): identify and verify customer and beneficial owner where applicable; understand relationship purpose/risk; maintain records; perform ongoing due diligence under the entity's regulatory framework.
Authentication: determine whether the person/device may perform a requested operation now (password, OTP, UPI PIN, biometric, cryptographic proof under a specific flow).
Authorization: product/bank/network decision to approve or decline a particular transaction or service operation.
Sanctions screening: compare relevant people/entities/payment details with applicable designated lists and legal restrictions.
AML/CFT monitoring: risk-based review of activity and patterns for potential money-laundering/terrorist-financing indicators; reporting duties apply to covered persons and facts as prescribed by PMLA/rules.
Fraud controls: detect deception, account takeover, stolen credentials, mule accounts or unauthorized payment behavior. AML and fraud overlap but are not identical.
Privacy/consent: legal basis and permitted use for personal/financial data; not equivalent to AML authority.

CDD WORKFLOW
1. Establish who is the regulated entity and customer/product relationship; use risk-based customer acceptance policy.
2. Collect required identity data using a permitted method; validate document/authenticity; identify beneficial owners for legal persons as applicable.
3. Screen required lists and risk markers; resolve plausible matches through documented process. Do not automatically accuse or permanently block solely from a fuzzy-name match.
4. Assign risk profile and apply onboarding limits/controls under current rule and entity policy.
5. Maintain source, verification time, reviewer and evidence; use CKYCR where applicable. CKYCR record availability does not replace entity-specific CDD obligations.
6. Refresh/monitor information and transactions on a risk-based basis; handle triggered review, missing data, periodic updates and customer notices under current applicable directions.

TRANSACTION MONITORING PIPELINE
Ingest transaction/account events -> normalize customer/entity/account identifiers -> enrich with expected customer activity and risk inputs -> run documented scenarios/rules/models -> create alert with reason/evidence -> triage false positive/needs info/escalate -> investigate linked activity -> make authorized decision -> file/report when required -> retain evidence and audit trail. Thresholds, rules, model features and reporting criteria are not universally public and should not be fabricated by the chatbot.

CASE AND REPORTING DATA
RegTech case should preserve customer/beneficial-owner identifiers, related accounts, transaction references, amounts/currencies, timestamps/value dates, counterparties, rule/model version, alert rationale, reviewer notes, source documents, decision and approvals. Access is restricted. STR/CTR and other reports follow current FIU-IND formats, covered-person definitions and prescribed deadlines; an alert is not itself a report, and filing is not proof of criminal conduct. Avoid disclosing confidential reporting to a subject where law prohibits tipping-off.

EXAMPLE: TRANSACTION REVIEW
A small business usually receives 20 predictable local credits. It suddenly receives many unrelated credits and quickly sends funds onward. A monitoring rule may create an alert. Analyst compares customer profile, payment references, counterparties, explanations, prior behavior and available records; system records rationale and escalation. Customer-facing status may simply say “under review.” The assistant cannot know whether a real bank ran this scenario or what decision it made.

SCREENING ENGINEERING CONTROLS
List version/effective date, transliteration, aliases, fuzzy threshold, country/name normalization, false-positive queue, dual approval for overrides, audit log and rerun procedure are important. Avoid hard-coding a single name similarity score as a legal determination. Screening takes place at required relationship/transaction points defined by applicable framework and entity policy.
