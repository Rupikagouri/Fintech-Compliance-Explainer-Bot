Compliance checks are the third stage of a digital payment. They are automated checks performed by financial institutions to ensure that a transaction follows legal and regulatory requirements.

What compliance checks:
1. AML (Anti-Money Laundering) — Screens the transaction and parties involved against known patterns of money laundering. Unusually large or structured transactions may be flagged.
2. KYC (Know Your Customer) — Verifies that the payer's identity has been established in accordance with regulations. Banks are required by law to verify the identity of their customers.
3. Sanctions screening — Checks the names and account details of the payer and payee against government and international sanctions lists (e.g., OFAC, UN, EU lists). Transactions involving sanctioned individuals or entities are blocked.
4. CFT (Counter-Terrorism Financing) — Checks for any link to terrorism financing activities.
5. Fraud detection — Automated systems analyze the transaction against typical behavior patterns for the account. An unusual transaction (wrong location, unusual time, large amount) may be flagged for additional review.
6. Transaction limits — Checks whether the transaction exceeds regulatory or bank-imposed limits (e.g., daily transfer limits, single transaction limits).
7. Reporting thresholds — Transactions above certain amounts may be automatically reported to regulators. In India, the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU-IND) receives reports of high-value transactions.

Why compliance checks sometimes cause delays:
Most compliance checks are automated and complete in milliseconds. However, if a transaction is flagged by an automated system, it may be placed in a queue for manual review by a compliance officer. This can take minutes, hours, or even days depending on the complexity.

The "Compliance check in progress" message typically means the automated systems are screening the transaction against these rules.

Is this normal?
Yes. Compliance checks happen on every transaction. The vast majority are cleared instantly. If you see a compliance-related message for an unusual period, it usually means the automated system has flagged something for a closer look. This is a safety feature, not necessarily a sign of wrongdoing.

What happens if a compliance check fails:
- The transaction is blocked or held.
- You may be contacted by your bank for additional documentation.
- Funds in transit may be returned to the original account.
- In serious cases, the matter may be reported to financial regulators.

Regulatory bodies involved in payment compliance:
- India: RBI (Reserve Bank of India), FIU-IND, SEBI
- USA: FinCEN, OFAC, Federal Reserve
- EU: EBA (European Banking Authority)
- International: FATF (Financial Action Task Force)
