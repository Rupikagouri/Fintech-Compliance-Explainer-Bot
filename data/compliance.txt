Financial Compliance: Rules That Govern Payment Transactions

WHAT IS COMPLIANCE IN PAYMENTS?
Financial compliance means following the laws, regulations, and rules that govern how money can be moved, stored, and reported. When a payment system performs a "compliance check," it is running automated tests to make sure a transaction does not break those rules.

Compliance is not optional. Regulated entities (banks, payment processors, wallets) must perform these checks or face large fines, loss of operating licences, or criminal charges.

KEY COMPLIANCE AREAS

AML – ANTI-MONEY LAUNDERING
Money laundering is the process of making illegally obtained money appear to come from a legitimate source. AML rules require financial institutions to:
- Monitor transactions for patterns that suggest laundering (e.g., many small deposits followed by one large withdrawal, called "structuring").
- Screen customers and transactions against watchlists.
- File Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) or Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs) with regulators when something looks wrong.
- Keep records for several years so transactions can be audited.

In India, the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) 2002 governs AML obligations. The Financial Intelligence Unit – India (FIU-IND) receives suspicious transaction reports.
Globally, the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) sets the international AML standards.

KYC – KNOW YOUR CUSTOMER
KYC is the process of verifying who your customer actually is before opening an account or allowing large transactions. Required information typically includes:
- Government-issued photo ID (passport, Aadhaar, PAN card in India, driver's licence).
- Proof of address.
- Date of birth.
- For businesses: company registration documents and details of beneficial owners.
KYC must be refreshed periodically and when a customer's risk profile changes.

In India, the RBI mandates KYC for all bank accounts. Accounts without complete KYC have restricted features.
The Aadhaar-based eKYC system allows digital KYC using biometric or OTP verification linked to the Aadhaar database.

CFT – COUNTER-FINANCING OF TERRORISM
CFT rules are similar to AML but focus on preventing money from reaching terrorist organisations. Payments to or from parties on government sanctions lists are blocked immediately.

SANCTIONS SCREENING
Sanctions are government or international restrictions on dealing with specific countries, entities, or individuals. Every payment must be screened against sanctions lists, including:
- OFAC (U.S. Office of Foreign Assets Control) lists.
- UN Security Council sanctions lists.
- EU restrictive measures lists.
- Country-specific lists (e.g., India's Ministry of Home Affairs designations).
A match results in the transaction being blocked or frozen and reported to the relevant authority.

PEP SCREENING – POLITICALLY EXPOSED PERSONS
A PEP is a person with a prominent public function (e.g., government official, senior executive of a state-owned enterprise, judge) or a close associate of such a person. Transactions involving PEPs require enhanced due diligence (EDD), meaning more scrutiny of the source of funds and more frequent monitoring.

TRANSACTION MONITORING
Banks and payment processors use automated systems to monitor all transactions in real time and over time. Triggers for investigation include:
- A transaction far above a customer's normal spending pattern.
- Large cash deposits just below reporting thresholds (structuring).
- Transactions with high-risk countries.
- Rapid movement of funds through multiple accounts (layering).

CTR – CASH TRANSACTION REPORT
In many countries, cash transactions above a threshold must be reported to the financial intelligence authority. In India, cash transactions above ₹10 lakh (1,000,000) in a single day must be reported to FIU-IND.

REGULATORY BODIES (INDIA)
- RBI (Reserve Bank of India): regulates banks and payment systems.
- SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India): regulates securities markets.
- IRDAI (Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India): regulates insurance.
- FIU-IND (Financial Intelligence Unit – India): receives and analyses suspicious transaction reports.
- NPCI (National Payments Corporation of India): operates UPI, NEFT, IMPS, and RuPay.

REGULATORY BODIES (GLOBAL)
- FATF (Financial Action Task Force): sets global AML/CFT standards.
- BIS (Bank for International Settlements): sets international banking standards (Basel accords).
- OFAC (Office of Foreign Assets Control, U.S.): administers U.S. sanctions.
- FinCEN (Financial Crimes Enforcement Network, U.S.): receives U.S. suspicious activity reports.

WHAT HAPPENS WHEN A COMPLIANCE CHECK FAILS?
Depending on the type of failure, the transaction may be:
- Automatically blocked (e.g., sanctions match).
- Placed "on hold" for manual review by a compliance analyst.
- Allowed to proceed but flagged for reporting (e.g., CTR filed).
Users whose transactions are blocked for compliance reasons will usually receive a message that the transaction "could not be processed" and are advised to contact their bank. The bank cannot always explain the specific reason for a block due to legal obligations around not "tipping off" the subject of a suspicious activity report.
