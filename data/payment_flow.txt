Digital Payment Transaction Flow: From Initiation to Settlement

OVERVIEW
A digital payment goes through five main stages before the money actually moves between accounts. Understanding each stage helps you know what is happening when your screen says "Verifying..." or "Processing...".

THE FIVE STAGES

1. PAYMENT INITIATION
The user triggers the payment. This can happen by:
- Tapping "Pay" in a UPI app (like PhonePe, Google Pay, or Paytm).
- Entering card details on an e-commerce website.
- Scanning a QR code at a shop.
- Setting up an automatic bank transfer.

At this point, no money has moved. The payment request is simply created.

2. VERIFICATION
The system confirms the transaction is legitimate. See also: verification.txt for full detail.
Key checks:
- Identity authentication (OTP, PIN, biometric).
- Account status (active, not blocked or frozen).
- Sufficient funds or credit limit.
- Fraud signals (unusual location, device, or amount).
If verification fails, the transaction is declined immediately and no money moves.

3. COMPLIANCE CHECKS
Automated compliance engines run the transaction against regulatory rules. This happens in real time, often in milliseconds. Checks include:
- AML (Anti-Money Laundering) screening: does the payment look like it could be laundering money?
- Sanctions screening: is either party on a government sanctions list?
- Transaction limits: does the amount exceed allowed daily or per-transaction limits?
- Source of funds flags: is the origin of the money consistent with the customer's profile?
If a compliance check flags the transaction, it may be blocked, held for review, or reported to a regulator.

4. PROCESSING
The payment instruction is routed between financial institutions.
- For card payments: the card network (Visa, Mastercard, RuPay) routes the message between the merchant's bank (acquirer) and the cardholder's bank (issuer).
- For UPI payments: NPCI routes the message between the sender's bank and the receiver's bank.
- For NEFT/RTGS: the Reserve Bank of India's systems handle the interbank transfer.
During processing, funds are typically "reserved" or "held" in the sender's account, not yet transferred.

5. SETTLEMENT
The actual movement of money between banks. This can happen at different speeds:
- Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS): individual high-value transfers settled immediately.
- Net settlement: banks batch up many transactions and settle the net difference at fixed times (e.g., NEFT in India settles in half-hourly batches).
- Card payments: typically settle one to three business days after the transaction.
- UPI: the underlying bank settlement typically completes within the same day.
After settlement, the funds are irrevocably transferred and the transaction is complete.

WHY DOES IT SOMETIMES TAKE TIME?
- Compliance checks may place a transaction "on hold" for manual review.
- Net settlement systems only run at certain intervals.
- Weekends and bank holidays can delay interbank settlement.
- International payments involve additional correspondent banking steps and may take one to five business days.

TRANSACTION STATUS MESSAGES
- "Verifying" – authentication and fraud checks are running.
- "Compliance check in progress" – AML/sanctions screening is running.
- "Processing" – the payment instruction is being routed.
- "Pending" – initiated but settlement not yet complete.
- "Settled" or "Successful" – money has moved, transaction complete.
- "Declined" – a check failed; no money moved.
- "On hold" – a compliance flag has paused the transaction; may need manual review.

PAYMENT RAILS
Different payment systems (called "rails") handle different types of transactions:
- UPI (Unified Payments Interface): India's real-time interbank retail payment system, operated by NPCI.
- NEFT (National Electronic Funds Transfer): batch-based interbank transfers in India.
- RTGS (Real-Time Gross Settlement): high-value, time-critical interbank transfers in India.
- IMPS (Immediate Payment Service): real-time 24/7 interbank transfers in India.
- SWIFT: international wire transfers between banks globally.
- Card networks (Visa, Mastercard, RuPay, Amex): facilitate card-based payments worldwide.
