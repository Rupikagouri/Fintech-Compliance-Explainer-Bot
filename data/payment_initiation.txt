Payment Initiation is the first stage of a digital payment transaction. It begins when a user submits a payment request through a digital channel such as a mobile banking app, a payment gateway, a UPI app, or an online checkout page.

During initiation, the following information is captured:
- Payer details (the person sending money)
- Payee details (the person or merchant receiving money)
- Amount and currency
- Payment instrument (debit card, credit card, UPI ID, bank account)
- Transaction reference or order ID

What happens during initiation:
1. The user confirms the payment details on the app or website.
2. The payment platform creates a transaction record with a unique transaction ID.
3. The request is sent securely to the payment processor or acquiring bank.
4. The system checks basic data validity (e.g., account number format, amount is positive).

Why it sometimes takes a moment:
The system must securely transmit your request to the right payment network. This includes encrypting your data and routing it to the correct institution. Even though this takes only a fraction of a second, network latency and server load can add a brief delay.

Payment initiation does not mean the money has moved. It is the start of a multi-stage process. The "Verifying..." message you see on screen usually means the system has received your request and is beginning the next steps.

Common payment initiation channels:
- UPI (Unified Payments Interface) in India
- Card payment at point-of-sale or online
- Net banking transfer
- Digital wallets (e.g., Paytm, PhonePe, Google Pay)
- NEFT, RTGS, or IMPS bank transfers

If initiation fails, the transaction is stopped before any money movement occurs. You would typically receive an error message such as "Invalid account number" or "Payment could not be initiated."
