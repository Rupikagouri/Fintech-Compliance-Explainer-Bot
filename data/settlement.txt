Settlement is the fifth and final stage of a digital payment transaction. It is the point at which money actually moves from the payer's account to the payee's account. Before settlement, the transaction is only authorized — the funds are reserved but not yet fully transferred.

The difference between authorization and settlement:
- Authorization: The issuing bank confirms the payer has sufficient funds and reserves that amount. The transaction is approved but money has not physically moved yet.
- Settlement: The actual transfer of funds between banks occurs. The payer's account is debited and the payee's account is credited.

Why settlement takes time:
For many payment types, authorization happens instantly but settlement is a separate process that occurs later, often at the end of the business day or in batches. This is because banks and financial institutions settle net positions with each other rather than processing each transaction individually.

Settlement timelines by payment type:
- UPI: Near-instant settlement in most cases.
- IMPS: Real-time settlement, 24/7.
- RTGS: Real-time, typically used for transactions above ₹2 lakh in India.
- NEFT: Settled in batches, multiple times per day during banking hours.
- Debit card payments: Typically 1-2 business days.
- Credit card payments: Typically 1-3 business days (the merchant receives funds after settlement).
- International wire transfers: 1-5 business days depending on the corridors and intermediary banks.

Gross vs. Net Settlement:
- Gross settlement: Each transaction is settled individually in real time (e.g., RTGS).
- Net settlement: Multiple transactions are netted against each other and settled as a single amount at a defined time (e.g., NEFT, card networks).

What "pending" means:
If a transaction shows as "pending" in your bank account, it usually means the authorization has been completed but settlement has not yet occurred. The funds may be reserved (deducted from your available balance) but the final debit may not appear in your statement until settlement.

Interbank settlement systems:
- India: RTGS and NEFT are operated by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI).
- USA: Fedwire, ACH (Automated Clearing House).
- Europe: TARGET2, SEPA.
- International: Correspondent banking through SWIFT.

Settlement failures:
Settlement failures are rare but can occur due to system outages, regulatory holds, or insufficient funds at the institutional level. If a settlement fails, the transaction is typically reversed and funds are returned to the payer.

Final settlement is the end of the payment lifecycle. Once settled, the transaction is complete and irreversible under normal circumstances.
