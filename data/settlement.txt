Payment Settlement: When Money Actually Moves

WHAT IS SETTLEMENT?
Settlement is the final stage of a payment transaction, when money is actually transferred from the payer's bank to the payee's bank. Before settlement, funds may be "authorised" or "reserved" but have not physically moved between bank accounts.

Think of it like writing a cheque (old analogy): the cheque is authorised when the recipient accepts it, but settlement only happens when the bank processes it and the money changes hands.

SETTLEMENT VS. AUTHORISATION
- Authorisation: the issuing bank confirms the funds are available and reserves them. The merchant or payee is promised payment.
- Clearing: the transaction details are exchanged between banks and verified.
- Settlement: the actual transfer of funds between banks; the transaction is complete.

SETTLEMENT TYPES

RTGS – REAL-TIME GROSS SETTLEMENT
Each transaction is settled individually and immediately, in real time. There is no batching.
- Used for: large-value, time-critical payments (typically above ₹2 lakh in India).
- Speed: immediate (within seconds during bank hours).
- Risk: because each payment is final immediately, there is no netting.
- In India: operated by the Reserve Bank of India; available 24x7 since December 2020.

NEFT – NATIONAL ELECTRONIC FUNDS TRANSFER (INDIA)
Transactions are collected into batches and settled at regular intervals.
- Used for: retail bank-to-bank transfers of any amount.
- Speed: settled in half-hourly batches, around the clock.
- In India: operated by NPCI/RBI; available 24x7.

IMPS – IMMEDIATE PAYMENT SERVICE (INDIA)
Real-time, 24x7 interbank transfers for retail amounts.
- Used for: immediate retail transfers up to ₹5 lakh (limit may vary by bank).
- Speed: immediate.
- Infrastructure: runs on NPCI's IMPS network.

UPI SETTLEMENT (INDIA)
UPI payments trigger IMPS in the background for the actual bank-to-bank settlement.
- Merchant settlements from UPI are typically credited to the merchant's account within one business day (T+1) or the same day, depending on the payment aggregator.

CARD PAYMENT SETTLEMENT
Card transactions follow a separate settlement cycle:
- T+0: transaction authorised; funds "reserved" on the cardholder's account.
- T+1 to T+3: the merchant's bank (acquirer) batches the day's transactions and submits them to the card network (Visa, Mastercard, RuPay).
- The card network routes settlement instructions to each issuing bank.
- Funds are transferred to the merchant's account, net of interchange fees.
- Cardholder's statement reflects the final settled amount.

SWIFT – INTERNATIONAL SETTLEMENTS
For cross-border payments, SWIFT (Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication) provides the messaging network.
- SWIFT does not move money directly; it sends payment instructions between correspondent banks.
- Settlement may involve one or more intermediary (correspondent) banks.
- Speed: typically one to five business days, depending on the currency corridor and compliance checks.
- Cost: higher than domestic payments due to correspondent banking fees and foreign exchange conversion.

NET SETTLEMENT vs. GROSS SETTLEMENT
- Gross settlement: each payment settled individually (RTGS model). Requires more liquidity.
- Net settlement: at the end of a settlement cycle, banks calculate the net amount they owe each other and only transfer the difference. Reduces liquidity needs but introduces settlement risk during the day.

SETTLEMENT FINALITY
A payment is "final" when the receiving bank has an irrevocable claim to the funds. Before finality:
- A payment can potentially be reversed (e.g., a fraud recall).
After finality:
- The payment is permanent; reversal is only possible through a separate refund transaction initiated by the payee.

FAILED SETTLEMENT
Reasons a settlement can fail include:
- The receiving account is closed or frozen.
- Technical failure in the payment network.
- Compliance hold placed after authorisation.
- Insufficient funds at the time of settlement (rare for pre-authorised transactions).
When settlement fails, the reserved funds are typically released back to the payer's account within one to three business days.

SETTLEMENT FOR MERCHANTS (E-COMMERCE)
When you pay a merchant online, the merchant does not receive your money instantly. The payment aggregator (e.g., Razorpay, PayU, Paytm Payment Gateway) collects the funds and settles to the merchant's bank account on a schedule (e.g., T+1 or T+2 working days). The settlement amount is the transaction amount minus the payment aggregator's fee (called the Merchant Discount Rate or MDR).
