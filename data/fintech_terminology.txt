FinTech Terminology: A plain-language guide to common terms in digital payments and financial technology.

PAYMENT SYSTEMS AND NETWORKS

UPI (Unified Payments Interface):
A real-time payment system developed by the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI). UPI allows users to transfer money instantly between bank accounts using a Virtual Payment Address (VPA), mobile number, or QR code. It operates 24/7 and supports both peer-to-peer and merchant payments. Examples of UPI apps: Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm, BHIM.

NEFT (National Electronic Funds Transfer):
A batch-based fund transfer system operated by the Reserve Bank of India. NEFT processes transactions in hourly batches during banking hours. It is suitable for non-urgent transfers between bank accounts within India.

RTGS (Real Time Gross Settlement):
A real-time fund transfer system for high-value transactions (minimum ₹2 lakh in India). Each transaction is settled individually and immediately. Operated by the RBI.

IMPS (Immediate Payment Service):
A 24/7 instant fund transfer service available round the clock, including holidays. Available through mobile banking, internet banking, and ATMs.

SWIFT (Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication):
An international messaging network used by banks to send payment instructions across borders. SWIFT does not transfer money directly; it transmits secure messages between financial institutions.

COMPLIANCE AND REGULATION TERMS

KYC (Know Your Customer):
A regulatory requirement that financial institutions must verify the identity of their customers before offering services. KYC typically involves collecting government-issued ID proof, address proof, and photograph. It helps prevent fraud and financial crimes.

AML (Anti-Money Laundering):
A set of laws, regulations, and procedures designed to prevent criminals from disguising illegally obtained funds as legitimate income. Financial institutions monitor transactions and report suspicious activity to regulators.

CFT (Countering the Financing of Terrorism):
Regulatory measures designed to prevent money from being used to fund terrorist activities. Financial institutions screen transactions and parties against terrorism watch lists.

Sanctions Screening:
The process of checking transaction parties against government-issued sanctions lists. Transactions involving sanctioned individuals, organizations, or countries are blocked. Lists include OFAC (USA), UN Security Council, and EU sanctions lists.

TECHNICAL TERMS

Payment Gateway:
A technology service that securely captures and transmits payment information from the customer to the payment processor. It acts as the bridge between the merchant's platform and the financial network.

Payment Processor:
A company that handles the technical routing of payment transactions between banks and payment networks. It manages authorization, processing, and sometimes settlement.

Acquiring Bank (Acquirer):
The financial institution that processes credit or debit card payments on behalf of a merchant. It receives the payment instruction and forwards it to the card network.

Issuing Bank (Issuer):
The bank that issued the customer's payment card or holds their account. It approves or declines the transaction based on the customer's account status and available balance.

PCI-DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard):
A set of security standards that organizations handling card payments must comply with to protect cardholder data.

Tokenization:
The process of replacing sensitive payment information (such as a card number) with a unique digital token. The token is used for transaction processing while the real card number is stored securely elsewhere, reducing the risk of data theft.

Encryption:
Converting payment data into a coded form that can only be read by authorized systems. Payments are encrypted in transit to prevent interception.

TRANSACTION STATES

Pending: The transaction has been initiated and authorized, but settlement has not yet occurred.
Processing: The payment is actively being routed between financial institutions.
Completed/Settled: The money has successfully moved from the payer's account to the payee's account.
Failed: The transaction did not complete. Reasons include insufficient funds, authentication failure, or network error.
Reversed/Refunded: A completed transaction that was subsequently cancelled and the funds returned.
On Hold/Flagged: The transaction has been held for additional review, typically by compliance systems.

REGULATORY BODIES

RBI (Reserve Bank of India): India's central bank and primary financial regulator. Regulates banks, payment systems, and monetary policy in India.
NPCI (National Payments Corporation of India): Operates UPI, NEFT, IMPS, RuPay, and other payment infrastructure in India.
SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India): Regulates securities markets and investment products in India.
FinCEN (Financial Crimes Enforcement Network): US bureau that collects and analyzes financial transactions to combat financial crime.
FATF (Financial Action Task Force): An international organization that sets global standards for combating money laundering and terrorism financing.
