Payment Verification: How Banks Confirm Transactions Are Legitimate

WHAT IS PAYMENT VERIFICATION?
Verification is the stage in which a bank or payment system confirms that a transaction should be allowed to proceed. It covers identity, account validity, and fraud checks.

AUTHENTICATION vs. AUTHORISATION vs. VERIFICATION
- Authentication: confirming who you are (e.g., OTP, PIN, fingerprint).
- Authorisation: confirming you have permission to make the transaction (e.g., sufficient balance).
- Verification: the broader process that includes both, plus fraud and compliance checks.

IDENTITY AUTHENTICATION METHODS

OTP (One-Time Password)
A short numeric code sent via SMS or generated by an authenticator app. Valid for a single transaction or a short time window. Used widely in UPI and online banking.

PIN (Personal Identification Number)
A secret numeric code known only to the account holder. Used at ATMs and card terminals.

Biometric Authentication
Uses fingerprint, face recognition, or iris scan to confirm identity. Increasingly used in mobile banking and UPI apps.

3D Secure (3DS)
An additional authentication layer for card payments online. After entering card details, the cardholder is redirected to their bank to confirm the transaction (via OTP or app notification). Examples: Verified by Visa, Mastercard SecureCode.

ACCOUNT AND TRANSACTION CHECKS

Balance / Credit Limit Check
The payer's bank checks that sufficient funds or credit are available to cover the transaction amount.

Account Status Check
The bank checks that both the payer's and payee's accounts are active, not frozen or closed.

Duplicate Transaction Check
Systems check whether the same transaction has been submitted more than once to prevent accidental double payments.

Beneficiary Validation
For bank transfers, the payee's name is matched against the account number. In India, the Penny Drop or IMPS verification confirms that the account exists and the name matches.

FRAUD DETECTION
Real-time fraud detection systems analyse transaction patterns and flag anomalies:
- A transaction from a new device or unusual location.
- A value much higher than the account's typical activity.
- Multiple rapid transactions in quick succession.
Flagged transactions may be blocked, delayed, or trigger an additional authentication challenge.

WHAT HAPPENS WHEN VERIFICATION FAILS?
If any check fails the transaction is declined. Common reasons:
- Incorrect PIN or OTP.
- Insufficient funds.
- Card or account blocked.
- Fraud flag triggered by unusual activity.
The user should receive an error message and, in most cases, can retry with the correct credentials or contact their bank for assistance.
