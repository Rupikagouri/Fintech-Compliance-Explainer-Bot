Verification is the second stage of a digital payment. After a payment is initiated, the system checks that the transaction is authentic and that the payer has the authority and funds to make it.

What verification checks:
1. Identity verification — Confirms the person initiating the payment is who they say they are. This may involve a PIN, password, OTP (One-Time Password), biometric scan (fingerprint or face recognition), or two-factor authentication (2FA).
2. Account validity — Checks that the payer's account exists and is active.
3. Sufficient balance or credit — Verifies that the payer has enough money or available credit to cover the transaction amount.
4. Payee account validity — Confirms the recipient's account number or UPI ID exists.
5. Card validity — For card payments, checks the card number, expiry date, and CVV (not stored; only checked during verification).

Two-factor authentication (2FA):
Many payment systems require two separate proofs of identity before proceeding. For example:
- Something you know (PIN or password)
- Something you have (OTP sent to your phone or hardware token)

Why verification might cause a delay or failure:
- Wrong PIN or OTP entered
- OTP not received (network issues)
- Insufficient account balance
- Account frozen or restricted
- Card expired or blocked
- Payee account closed or invalid

The "Verifying..." message you see on your screen means the payment system is running these checks. This stage protects you from unauthorized use of your account.

Verification is performed by your bank or payment provider. The results are communicated back to the payment processor before the transaction continues to the compliance stage.

If verification fails, the transaction is declined and no money moves. You will usually receive a notification such as "Transaction declined - Insufficient funds" or "Authentication failed."
